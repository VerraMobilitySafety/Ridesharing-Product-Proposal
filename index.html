The current problem

Today, safety is fragmented.

Mobility platforms are solving many of the same safety problems independently — with different systems, definitions, teams, thresholds and response models.

Detection is inconsistentSome events are detected automatically. Others still depend on somebody being able to ask for help.
Response capability variesThe quality and speed of safety response can differ by operator, market, time and available resources.
Safety infrastructure is duplicatedOperators separately fund technology, people, processes, investigations and assurance capability.
Data speaks different languagesDifferent taxonomies, severity models and reporting structures make industry-level comparison difficult.
Intelligence stays in silosPatterns, lessons and substantiated serious-risk information do not easily move across platforms.
The opportunity is to connect detection, people, response, management and intelligence through one shared safety operating layer.

External pressure is increasing.

The operating model is being challenged from two directions at once: how platforms manage their workforce, and how much consequential decision-making can safely be automated.

Workforce model pressure↔

Employment status is under growing scrutiny.

Platform-worker classification continues to face legal and regulatory pressure. In the EU, employment status must be assessed from the reality of how work is performed, with a rebuttable presumption of employment where facts indicate direction and control.

Why it matters: shared safety capability can reduce duplicated operational infrastructure without pretending to remove each platform's own legal responsibilities or employment-status obligations.
Source: EU Platform Work Directive 2024/2831
Automation and human oversightAI

Automation needs meaningful human judgement.

In August 2026, the Dutch Data Protection Authority fined Uber €824.99 million over automated individual decisions affecting drivers. The case reinforces the need for transparency, governance and meaningful human involvement in consequential decisions.

Why it matters: the model should use AI to detect, triage and accelerate safety response — while trained people retain appropriate oversight and decision-making.
Source: CNIL summary of Dutch DPA decision, 24 Aug 2026
The opportunity

Detection matters. So does everything that follows.

The opportunity is to manage the full safety lifecycle — detecting risk earlier, deterring escalation, deploying assistance, responding consistently, managing incidents end-to-end and learning across the industry.

01 — Detect

See the event before someone has to report it.

Use technology and behavioural signals to identify potential safety events earlier — including situations where nobody can safely press SOS.

    Earlier visibility • earlier intervention
    Proposed solutions

    One safety operating layer. Seven connected capabilities.

    The opportunity is not one feature or one piece of technology. It is a shared operating model that can detect risk, support people, manage incidents and turn industry-wide experience into better prevention.

    01 — DETECT + DETER

    Sometimes nobody can press SOS.

    Use AI, telematics, cameras, audio and discreet distress signals to identify potential safety events earlier — then deploy an appropriate deterrent or escalate for human assessment.
